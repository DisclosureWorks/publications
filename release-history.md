### EFTA II Memo — v2.8.7 to v2.9.3

EFTA means Epstein Files Transparency Act; this memo examines the proposed second Act.

- The date line now keeps September 20, 2026 as the original date and adds “updated through October 30, 2026,” making the later material easier to place.
- A new list names sources cited but not held. The Government Accountability Office response is known only through a senator's April 28 press release, and a Politico article is known only through the senators' letter. The statute citation is also listed because the version held predates a renumbering.
- The searchability discussion now uses the October 30 catalogue query: roughly 1.39 million documents, with roughly 30% lacking searchable text. The note identifies the database’s modification date and says that the earlier July 30 catalogue state could not be reproduced from the records checked.
- The Phang court-case update adds the government’s later challenge to whether there was a final agency action and the October 28 denial of a stay pending appeal. It makes clear that the earlier concession applied to preliminary relief and that court orders do not establish completed public release.
- The audit discussion now reports that the Government Accountability Office accepted the senators’ request, attributing that update to a Senate press release. It makes the argument about unexplained record withdrawals conditional on the letter being right.
- The corrected note on press reports separates the senators’ claim of thousands of unexplained removals from what the articles say. It explains that the cited New Republic article gives no such count, while the British Broadcasting Corporation article reports thousands of removals alongside an explanation concerning exposed victims’ information.
- The comparison of the House and Senate bills now explains its counting method: 14 changed provisions, or 12 with the title and heading changes grouped together. It distinguishes three changes to operative wording from the title’s framing and describes the court-location wording as open to argument.
- The discussion of a congressional committee's subpoena suit now says the committee had standing, meaning the right to bring the case, to seek enforcement. The earlier text said the committee enforced the subpoena.
- No recommendation in the memo changed.

### National Security Power and Constitutional Imbalance — v8.20.4 to v9.2.2

- This edition changes the paper's stated scope. The previous version said the Epstein Files Transparency Act (EFTA) was included only because the executive branch holds the records, not because of any intelligence operation. This version keeps that ground and adds a second: a documented but unresolved question about an intelligence link, examined in the new Appendix D. The paper says the records do not establish an intelligence operation, and its disclosure measurements do not depend on the answer.
- Appendix D organizes its assessment under eleven headings and separates documented contacts, requests and investments from claims about their purpose. It treats repeated copies of the same material as one observation, considers ordinary explanations, and assigns no overall probability or score.
- The new passport discussion in Appendix D sets out the evidence that a passport in another name was held, beside the competing accounts from prosecutors and defense counsel. It separates possession from who made the journeys, how the document was obtained and why it was kept, and it does not present intelligence use as established.
- Bibliography entries now carry a visible label where the paper holds no copy, only a summary or abstract, or only part of a work (for example, two of three folders of one report).
- In the historical cases, the House's January 1976 vote on releasing the Pike committee report now follows the text of the amendment in the Congressional Record (release restricted to authorized recipients or certified by the President). The 1986 Iran arms-deal entry now says only what the translated magazine report states, and notes that the Arabic original is not held. Statements about professional discipline now read "the record reviewed shows no discipline" instead of implying a complete record.
- The Phang litigation timeline extends through October 28 and separates the government’s arguments, submissions for a judge’s private inspection and appellate orders from rulings on the merits or completed public production. Earlier references to conceded arguments are limited to the preliminary-injunction stage.
- The page-count discussion now describes 2,729,256 as the sum of known page counts and states that three documents have no recorded count. It retains separate totals for different analyses and removes the blanket description of their differences as bookkeeping rather than measurement.
- The notes correct the earlier description of 2,571 additional identifiers in an independent public index as empty placeholders. They now say that those entries have image paths and that their release and content status remains unresolved.
- No theory rating or score in the paper changed.
- Privacy policy changed: personal data in the record is now published, except for victims, survivors and minors, and medical information about private individuals the record does not name as taking part in the conduct described. See the redaction policy and the corrections and removal policy.
